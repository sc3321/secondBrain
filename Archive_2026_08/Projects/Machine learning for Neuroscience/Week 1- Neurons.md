Resting membrane potential: 

A neuronal membrane separates the inside and outside of the cell. There is movement of ions through the membrane due to chemical and electrical gradients which are created. These ions reach an equilibrium known as Nernst Potential (~ -70mV) where generally:
    There is higher K+ distribution inside
    Na+ and Cl- distributions higher outside.

Movement of ions through the membrane mediated by:
    Passive channels as a result of diffusion.
    Active channels which require energy and move ions through specific channels against their concentration gradient.


Spikes: Action potentials which are responsible for information transmission across the neurons.

General process:
    
    1) Neurons recieve input (from another neuron?)
    2) Na+ channels open allowing more Na+ into the cell which increases the potential inside the membrane. This process is called depoloraization.
    3) If this membrane potential increases above a threshold, which would only happen in cases of a substantial input signal, VOLTAGE GATED Na+ channels open in a positive feedback loop to allow more Na+ to enter the neuron resulting in a peak positive potential.
    4) At this peak, Na+ channels close but K+ channels open. K+ are already in high concentration INSIDE the neuron, so by opening these channels the neuron membrane potential decreases as K+ ions leave the cell. This process is called repolarization.
    5) What is interesting is that the membrane potential is decreased to BELOW its resting membrane potential to ~ -90mV. This phenomenon titled hyperpolarization, creates a refractory period where it is systematically harder to triger the neuron to fire another spike.
