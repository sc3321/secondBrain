a The mechanisms underlying Ethereum 

	i) Explain how Proof of Stake (PoS) works in the context of Ethereum. Consider how new blocks are proposed, how network participants reach consensus and how the honesty of network participants is incentivised. 

		PoS is the consensus algorithm adopted by the Ethereum network. Any node on the network can stake a minimum of 32 ETH to be eligible to be a validator. Every slot ~ 12s, a new proposer is chosen at random from this pool of potential validators. This proposer will choose the transactions from its local mempool to include in a block and execute these transactions and propose a new block with a block header that contains a stateRoot value as a result of the transactions it has executed. The other nodes act as validators who perform the same transactions and compare if they arrive at the same stateRoot. Any incorrect behaviour and either the validator or the proposer can have their stake slashed, with a potential attacker needing to control over 2/3 of the staked ETH per slot to tamper with a transaction and have a nefarious transaction commited. Validators are awarded for detecting this false behaviour and for correct attestations. 
	
	ii) Explain the difference between Externally Owned Accounts (EOAs) and contract accounts. Which of these can initiate transactions and why? 

	EOA's are like account holders. Their address is generated from a private-public key pair. This is needed, because the private key is used to sign off on a transaction to make it valid, otherwise it will be rejected by nodes on the ETH network. Every address has an ETH balance, and this EOA has the private key which controls access to the funds which is needed to pay gas fees, make transactions etc. Contract accounts are deployed by EOA's or other contract accounts, but every transaction must originate from an EOA. Contract accounts can execute code and even call other contract accounts, but the original transaction to set things in motion will always be called by an EOA.

	iii) What is the gas mechanism, how does it work, and why is it needed? 

	Gas is like the currency of computation in Ethereum. Every OPCODE that the EVM executes has an associated gas cost, proportional to the complexity of the opcode itself. This is important to prevent DoS attacks with transactions running in an infinite loop stopping the network. This also means that nodes do not have to waste a lot of their compute and this will be rewarded with higher gas fees.

b Decentralised Finance (DeFi) and Automated Market Makers (AMMs) 

	i) What is the difference between custodial and non-custodial stablecoins? Discuss the risks associated with each type. 

		Custodial stablecoins are coins which have a third party- usually off chain, who manages the collateral. They have the advantage that they are easily pegged to some physical currency, say CBDC; central bank digital currencies which are issued by governments. This easy peg-keeping makes them less volatile and easy to swap with. Non custodial stablecoins use on-chain mechanisms to regulate the price of the stablecoin. They usually do this by means of an oracle. Failures are due to the obvious failure of an oracle and in the collapse of an on-chain asset used as collateral.
	
	ii) State what an Automated Market Maker is and comment on why it is the most common means of exchange in DeFi applications. 

	An AMM replaces the general order book architecture of trading off-chain. Order books would be very gas intensive as this is a complex look-up and matching process. Instead with AMM's, users trade against an existing liquidity pool which is dynamically prices based on the liquidity of the 2 tokens in the AMM. Different techniques like constant sum or product invariant, dynamically prices the 2 assets against each other based on their relative liquidities. It allows different users to provide liquidity to these AMM's and share in a proportion of the borrow fees that the AMM issues.

c Overcollateralisation and Liquidations

	i) Overcollateralisation of loans is a key design feature of DeFi lending protocols. Explain how this boosts the economic security of such protocols. 

		When borrowing an asset, the borrower must provide collateral which can be claimed by the lender if the value of the collateralised asset falls by a certain amount in comparison to the value of the debted asset. This collateralised value is usually greater than the value of the debted asset, and is a means of protecting the lender. If the collateralised object falls too much in value in relation to the debted asset, then the collateral can be siezed immediately by the lender and sold off at a discount to other nodes who will claim the collateral at a risk free discount and pay the lender a nominal fee.
